# Channel brief: Built & Broken

## The promise
The rise and fall of America's great builds and businesses. Every video tells
one true American story with a clear arc: an ambitious plan, the people who
drove it, the money, the peak, the trouble, the fall, and what is left today.

## Two kinds of story, one arc
- **Business stories (about 60%)**: companies, brands, industries and fortunes.
  Examples: why Montgomery Ward collapsed, the Studebaker story, the Pullman
  company town.
- **Megaproject stories (about 40%)**: dams, canals, bridges, highways,
  railroads, towers, boomtowns. Examples: the Erie Canal's boom and bust, the
  towns the Interstate killed, the Hoover Dam's human cost.

The arc is the same in both. The audience comes for the arc.

## Who is watching
Curious adults, mostly 25 to 60, mostly American, who enjoy history,
business and engineering. They watch documentaries on their TV in the evening
and stay for 10 to 15 minutes when the story keeps turning. They are smart,
not specialists. They hate fluff, clickbait that does not pay off, and being
lectured.

## What makes this channel different
- **Period footage.** The visuals are real films from the time: corporate
  films, government films, newsreels. The viewer sees the actual era.
- **Primary sources.** Every video quotes people and newspapers from the time,
  word for word, with the paper and the date.
- **Numbers with meaning.** Money is given in the original figure and today's
  equivalent. Sizes are compared to things the viewer knows.
- **A point of view.** Each video ends with a judgement on why it rose and why
  it fell. This is the channel's voice, and it is what keeps the channel
  original rather than mass-produced.

## Tone
Calm, confident, a little wry. A smart friend telling you a story they
researched properly. Never breathless, never shouting, never "you won't
believe".
