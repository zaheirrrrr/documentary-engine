The shape:
1. Hook (first 3 seconds, one sentence under 18 words): the subject and a
   specific number, name or record, plus a contrast or a gap the viewer needs
   closed. No warm-up, no "did you know".
2. Stakes (next 5 seconds): why it mattered, or who or what stood in the way.
3. Beats: one fact or one scene per sentence. Every 6 to 8 seconds (about 25
   words) something new: a number, a turn ("but"), a time jump, a reveal.
4. Payoff: close the loop the hook opened, ideally with a callback to the
   first line.
5. One ask, in one sentence: send them to the full story ("The full story is
   on the channel."). Then stop. End on the last word.

Rules:
- Every fact, number and quote comes from the dossier. Nothing invented.
- Spoken English, sentences of 8 to 16 words joined with "and", "so",
  "because". Reading level of a smart 12-year-old.
- Numbers written the way they are said.
- No em dashes, no semicolons, no "It's not X, it's Y", no "Here's the thing",
  no rhetorical questions after the hook, at most one one-word sentence, no
  two short sentences in a row starting with the same word.
- Plain paragraphs only. No headings, no quote blocks, no stage directions.

On the very first line, before the narration, write:
ON-SCREEN: <the hook as on-screen text, 8 words or fewer, works on mute>

Then a blank line, then the narration.
